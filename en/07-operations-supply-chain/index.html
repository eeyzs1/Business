Operations & Supply Chain

Marketing makes the promise; operations keeps it. This branch gives you a pair of shop-floor eyes: from the process behind a cup of milk tea to the tremors running through a global supply chain — cost, speed, and reliability are all born here.

1Sub-branch Map

Operations and supply chain management studies two sides of one question: how to reliably turn resources into value in the customer’s hands. Inside a company it is called operations; between companies it is called the supply chain. The table below is a six-piece map of the field: this branch covers five of the six in four lessons, leaving project management for later, more advanced study.

2Learning Path: Why This Order

Inside Out: From a Cup of Milk Tea to a Global Chip Shortage

Process language → Bottleneck leverage → Quality and lean → Cross-company coordination

First learn to read a process — it is the grammar of the operations world, with activities, inventory, and cycle time all laid out on the diagram. Then find the bottleneck — the system’s only leverage point for output; improving anywhere else is mostly wasted effort. Next come quality and lean — relentlessly eliminating waste around value, so that good no longer depends on luck. Finally pull the camera back to the supply chain — you will see how processes between companies amplify each other’s chaos, and you will understand where the chip shortage came from.

How to study Each lesson takes 30–45 minutes: play with the interactive simulator first to build intuition, then read the main text to upgrade that intuition into concepts, and finish with the quiz as retrieval practice. The four simulators form one continuous story: the three-station tea shop of Lesson 1 grows into a five-station line in Lesson 2.
