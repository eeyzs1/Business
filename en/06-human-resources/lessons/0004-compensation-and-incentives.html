Human Resources · Lesson 4

Compensation and Incentives

Money is not the only motivator — but a badly designed pay structure is enough to destroy every other one. This lesson takes apart the design logic of paying people: 3P pay, total rewards, the fixed-variable mix, and equity incentives — with a single goal: employees who feel secure and still strive.

1Pay Design Answers Three Questions at Once

In companies of any size, complaints about pay always arrive from three directions. Is it fair inside (same work — how large is the gap in pay)? Is it competitive outside (compared with the market — am I losing out)? Does it motivate this person (does income actually differ by effort and by result)? These three questions map onto the classic framework of pay design — 3P pay: paying for the position (the value of the role itself), for performance (the results delivered), and for the person (the scarcity of their knowledge and skills). Most roles’ pay is a weighted blend of the three — only the weights differ: production lines weight the position, sales weights performance, R&D weights the person.

Concept: 3P Pay · Pay for Position / Performance / Person

The position sets the band (what this role is worth), performance sets where you sit inside the band (strong results pull you upward), and the person sets the scarcity premium (skills nobody can replace command extra). The three logics complement one another; paying on any single dimension alone will skew.

2Total Rewards and the Fixed-Variable Mix: The Knobs of Security and Drive

What an employee receives goes far beyond the monthly salary. Widening the lens gives the total rewards view:

Total rewards = fixed cash + variable cash + benefits + growth and experience

The fixed part buys security, the variable part buys drive, and benefits and growth buy belonging and a future. The most important knob among them is the fixed-variable mix (the ratio of fixed to variable pay). In sales roles, output is quantifiable and performance varies person to person, so pay is usually low-fixed and high-variable — income swings widely with results, and the best performers face no cap. In staff functions, output is collaborative and hard to quantify month by month, so pay is usually high-fixed and low-variable — stability first, driven by growth and recognition. There is no standard answer — only a fit with “how quantifiable the output is” and “people’s appetite for risk.” The simulator below hands you the knob to turn yourself.

3Internal Equity and External Competitiveness

“Fair inside” rests on job evaluation: scoring roles on scope of responsibility, complexity, impact, and qualifications (the point-factor method, for example; indicative), then slotting them into a grade ladder — same grade, same band. The psychological intuition underneath: employees are far more sensitive to “unfairness” than to “how much” — change the reference point, and the same salary carries a different feeling (Adams’s equity theory; see Branch 05, Management). “Competitive outside” rests on market percentiles: benchmark against salary-survey data — anchoring at P50 (the market median) is a “match” strategy, anchoring at P75 or above is a “lead” strategy, and paying a premium for scarce, critical roles is common practice (indicative). The tension between the two: inside, grades and bands rule; outside, the going rate rules. The usual resolution is “totals follow the market, structure follows the grades,” plus a tolerated “exception for scarce roles.”

4Equity and Options: Letting Employees Share the Residual

Wages buy labor; equity buys “thinking like an owner”: binding employees’ gains to the company’s long-term residual. The standard mechanics of a stock option (indicative): a set exercise price, vesting gradually over four years, usually with a one-year cliff — leave before you are vested and the options lapse, which is why they are jokingly called “golden handcuffs.” The cost is just as plain: if the company does not make it, the options are worth zero. So in a healthy design, equity is “the amplifier of the upside” and cannot substitute for “the floor of security” — paying an entire salary in options means the employee carries founder-level risk while holding only an employee’s single vote.

5Case Study: Huawei’s Virtual Restricted Shares and Startup Options

Two ways of sharing the residual

Huawei (illustrative): the company has long run an employee stock-ownership plan; employees hold “virtual restricted shares” — entitled to dividends and appreciation in value, but without full shareholder rights, not transferable to outsiders, and bought back by the company at departure. The logic is to let “strivers” share the fruits of the company’s growth, binding personal gain deeply to long-term performance. Startups (illustrative): with cash scarce, they top up total rewards with options — a lower fixed salary plus a grant that vests over four years. This naturally selects for people “willing to trade certainty for possibility”: if the company appreciates, early members earn a return far beyond what cash alone would have paid; if it fails, the options are worth zero and only the lower salary remains.

The lesson: the two share one core — “trading a share of tomorrow’s residual for today’s effort.” The difference is that Huawei shares already-validated cash flows, while startups share unvalidated possibility — the higher the certainty, the more expensive the “golden handcuffs” can be.

Key Terms

3P Pay
The framework that blends pay for position, performance, and person.
Total Rewards
The full package of fixed cash, variable cash, benefits, and growth experience.
Fixed-Variable Mix
The ratio of fixed pay to variable pay; the core knob balancing security and drive.
Job Evaluation
Scoring and ranking roles on responsibility, complexity, and other factors to support internal equity.
Market Percentile
Where pay sits in the market data — P50 to match, P75 to lead (indicative).
Equity Incentive
Arrangements that let employees share the company’s long-term residual through equity-type instruments.
Stock Option
The right to buy shares at a set exercise price in the future, usually vesting over four years.
Vesting
The schedule on which options gradually become yours; leave unvested and they lapse — the “golden handcuffs.”
Key Takeaways

Pay design answers three questions at once: fair inside (job value), competitive outside (market percentile), motivating for the person (performance and capability). The fixed-variable mix is a single knob for incentive and risk: low fixed and high variable buys drive — and buys volatility. Equity trades tomorrow’s residual for today’s effort: it is an amplifier of the upside, not a substitute for the floor.
