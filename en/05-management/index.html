Business · Branch 05 · Management

Management

The discipline of getting results through other people. Working alone takes nothing more than diligence; when many people work together, diligence is no longer enough — you need management: setting direction, dividing the work, igniting effort, and watching for deviations. This branch breaks a manager’s job into four puzzle pieces you can understand and learn to practice.

Management is a big house. The 4 lessons in this branch prioritize the four foundations that are used most often and transfer most readily to any industry; the remaining sub-branches are marked advanced for later — extend into them as needed once you have finished this branch.

2Learning Path: Why This Order

From Tasks to People, from Framework to Craft

Lesson 1 The Four Functions → Lesson 2 Decision-Making → Lesson 3 Motivation → Lesson 4 Teams and Leadership

First you get the full skeleton of managerial work (the four functions). Then you step into a manager’s most everyday and most error-prone activity — decision-making — and see the mind’s systematic biases clearly. Next the camera moves inside the person: where motivation comes from. Finally the individuals assemble into a whole: how teams evolve and how leadership flexes to the person. Strung together, the four lessons form the complete chain of “set direction → make the call → ignite people → lead the team.”

3Lessons

  • What Is Management? The Four Functions The planning → organizing → leading → controlling loop; efficiency versus effectiveness; Mintzberg’s managerial roles. Interactive: the POLC cycle wheel.
  • Decision-Making: Rationality and Biases Bounded rationality and satisficing; anchoring, confirmation bias, sunk costs, loss aversion. Interactive: the bias lab (three mini-experiments).
  • Motivation: Why People Are Willing to Work Hard Maslow’s five levels, Herzberg’s two factors, and the three-link chain of expectancy theory. Interactive: a two-factor sorting game + expectancy-chain toggles.
  • Teams and Leadership Tuckman’s five stages; task and relationship behaviors; the four styles of situational leadership; leadership vs management. Interactive: a situational leadership matrix simulator.
