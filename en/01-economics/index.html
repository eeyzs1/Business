Branch 01 · Economics

Economics

Limited resources, unlimited wants — every person and every firm must choose under this constraint. Economics is the operating system of the business world: once you can read prices, you can read how firms, industries, and whole economies work.

1Sub-branch Map

Economics splits into two sub-branches: microeconomics looks at a single market — how the price of one good forms; macroeconomics looks at the whole economy — how a nation’s output, prices, and employment interact. The table below is the complete map of this branch, and which lesson covers each part.

2Learning Path: Why This Order

Zooming out: one good → revenue → an industry → the whole economy

Lesson 1 Supply and Demand (how the price of one good forms)→ Lesson 2 Elasticity (what happens to revenue when the price moves)→ Lesson 3 Market Structures (zoom out to the industry: who has pricing power)→ Lesson 4 Macroeconomics (zoom all the way out: a nation’s thermometer and thermostat)

Micro before macro: without the price of a single market you cannot understand the CPI; without elasticity you cannot understand why rate hikes tame inflation. Each lesson takes 30–45 minutes and includes interactive simulations and an instant-feedback quiz.

3Lessons

  • Supply and Demand: How Prices Emerge Drag the price line to create a surplus or a shortage with your own hands, and watch the market self-correct.
  • Elasticity: Why Raising Prices Doesn’t Always Raise Revenue Drag the elasticity slider and watch the same price hike meet two very different fates — in the grain market and the iPhone market.
  • Market Structures: From Perfect Competition to Monopoly Slide along the spectrum from a farmers’ market to the national power grid, with a radar chart updating across four competitive settings.
  • Macroeconomics: GDP, Inflation, and Interest Rates A flowing circular-flow diagram plus a step-by-step rate-hike transmission chain — learn to read the macro news.

4Connections to Other Branches

Prerequisites: none

Economics is the foundation of the entire curriculum — it requires no prerequisites, yet it supplies the vocabulary for every course that follows: costs in accounting, interest rates in finance, pricing in marketing, and competitive analysis in strategy are all, at bottom, stories about supply, demand, and incentives.
