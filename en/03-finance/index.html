Branch 03 · Finance

Finance: The Time and Price of Money

Accounting shows you what a business looks like now; finance tells you what its future is worth. This branch answers three questions: how do you convert today’s money into next year’s, and back? Why must risk be compensated? And how do you price “is it worth it” — for a project, a stock, a country’s financial system?

1Sub-branch Map

Finance is a big tree. This branch’s four lessons walk the most essential stretch of its four main limbs: what you cover gets a check mark; the rest is left as signposts.

2Learning Path: Why This Order

The spine: first fit money with its two extra dimensions — time and risk — then zoom out to firms and markets

① Time value→② Risk and return→③ NPV decisions→④ Market map

First learn to convert “future money” into “today’s money” (Lesson 1); then see why the conversion must charge extra for risk (Lesson 2); the two streams merge in Lesson 3 into NPV, the core tool of corporate investment decisions; finally the camera pulls back to the market and institutional network where these trades happen, and to how interest rates reach the real economy (Lesson 4). The first two lessons are the parts, the third is the assembly, the fourth is the map.

4Connections to Other Branches

Prerequisites: 02 Accounting — financial statements are the raw material of valuation

Every forecast of “future cash flows” in finance starts from the accountant’s statements: the income statement sketches the cash flows, the balance sheet shows how much capital is tied up. You can study this branch without 02 Accounting, but come back after it and the cash-flow work in Lesson 3 will go far more smoothly.
