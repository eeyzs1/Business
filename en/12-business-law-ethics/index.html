Branch 12 · Business Law & Ethics

Business Law & Ethics

Markets reward cleverness — and punish overreach. This branch is the foundation and guardrail of the entire curriculum: law draws the lines you must not cross, while ethics asks what comes after legality — whether you can do better. The four lessons follow one through-line: where does trust in commercial society come from? The answers arrive in turn: a defined identity (your choice of business entity), promises put in writing (contracts), power that checks power (corporate governance), and a scoreboard longer than the quarterly profit (ethics and ESG).

Important note Every page in this branch that touches on the law is a teaching illustration, not legal advice: statutes are amended over time, and for specific situations please consult a licensed attorney or other qualified professional.

1Sub-branch Map

In the business-school canon, “business law and ethics” spans three fields: business law, corporate governance, and business ethics. This is not about memorizing statutes — it is about understanding the rules layer of the business world: what each player is allowed to do, what makes a promise enforceable, who watches the powerful, and who guards the bottom line.

2Learning Path: Identity → Commitment → Checks & Balances → Direction

Why this order

Lesson 1 sets the identity: choosing a legal form is choosing a whole package of default rules for liability and financing → Lesson 2 puts promises in writing: contracts turn verbal trust into enforceable agreements → Lesson 3 installs checks and balances: governance keeps the people who manage your money from straying → Lesson 4 sets the direction: ethics and ESG decide how far the company deserves to go

The four concepts run from hard to soft, from external to internal: legal form and contracts are the “hard constraints,” governance and ethics the “soft” ones. Yet the softer the constraint, the bigger the reward for honoring it — trust is the only intangible asset that compounds.
